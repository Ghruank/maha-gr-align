Every figure in this report was measured on the real corpus and is reproducible by a command
named in Section~\ref{sec:repro}.

Beyond the specification, the system implements two checks the SRS does not ask for and which
address the problem statement's ``cross-department conflict detection'' more directly than
similarity search can:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=6mm,itemsep=1mm]
\item \textbf{Cross-document contradiction} --- a draft that restates a figure from the order it
      cites, differently from what that order says, is caught exactly. Demonstrated by two
      documents that each pass every other check.
\item \textbf{Blast radius} --- before a draft that cancels an order is issued, the system reports
      how many GRs still in force depend on that order, and in which departments.
\end{itemize}

\section{Functional requirements}

\subsection*{3.1 Reference Parser Module}

\begin{longtable}{@{}p{11mm}p{15mm}p{125mm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Req} & \textbf{Verdict} & \textbf{Evidence} \\
\midrule
\endhead
FR-1 & \MET &
\code{references.py} extracts cited orders from the preamble and the operative body, and
classifies nine document kinds: Government Resolution, circular, corrigendum, notification,
order, \textbf{court order}, Act, letter, minutes. The distinction is load-bearing --- the
archive holds departmental orders and never holds court orders, so classifying before judging
is what stops a High Court order being reported as a missing GR. \\
\addlinespace
FR-2 & \MET &
Each citation resolves against the 17{,}283-node index on two keys: the Devanagari file number
(exact, or a script-independent digit key) \emph{and} the date within a $\pm$3-day window.
Neither alone suffices --- 41.1\% of GRs share a file number, and a date-only resolver produced
19{,}840 edges with five GRs falsely tied at in-degree 292. \\
\addlinespace
FR-3 & \MET &
Thirteen finding codes with line numbers and click-to-highlight in the document. \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\textbf{Measured extraction: 98.9\%} of date-bearing reference items (3{,}461 of 3{,}501 across
906 GRs), against an acceptance threshold of 80\%. Reaching it required supporting spelled-out
Marathi dates --- {\dev दिनांक ०९ जुलै, २०२६} --- matched on a matra-stripped skeleton so that a
visually-reordered PDF text layer still parses correctly.

\subsection*{3.2 Semantic Conflict Detector}

\begin{longtable}{@{}p{11mm}p{15mm}p{125mm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Req} & \textbf{Verdict} & \textbf{Evidence} \\
\midrule
\endhead
FR-4 & \MET &
\code{embed.py} extracts operative clauses and encodes them with MahaSBERT
(\code{l3cube-pune/marathi-sentence-similarity-sbert}, 768-dim, CPU). Only the operative section
is indexed: headers and {\dev वाचा} blocks are near-identical corpus-wide and would make every
GR the nearest neighbour of every other. \\
\addlinespace
FR-5 & \MET &
23{,}977 clauses from 6{,}000 GRs; 21{,}018 searchable after masking procedural boilerplate.
Search excludes the drafting officer's own department by default, which is the requirement's
point --- within a department the officer already knows the standing orders. Measured live:
\textbf{0.6\,s} per draft. \\
\addlinespace
FR-6 & \MET &
Each hit shows the cosine score, a band, the source department and date, and a link that opens
the source GR with its supersession status and its official \code{gr.maharashtra.gov.in} URL. \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\textbf{Retrieval quality.} Procedural furniture --- the Finance-concurrence line, the budget-head
line --- appears near-verbatim in a large share of all GRs and matched everything before it was
masked, at scores of 0.87--0.91. With 2{,}959 such clauses (12\%) excluded, hits are substantive:
an infrastructure-funding draft returns other departments' infrastructure-funding clauses at
0.66--0.69, and a fund-release draft returns fund-release clauses at 0.71--0.76. The masking is
applied at \emph{search} time rather than index time, so the exclusion list can be tightened
without re-encoding.

Similarity is reported as a score against a stated threshold rather than as a verdict, and every
hit is presented as a candidate for the officer to read. This matches FR-6's wording --- the
requirement asks for \emph{potential} conflicts.

\subsection*{3.3 Bilingual Terminology Module}

\begin{longtable}{@{}p{11mm}p{15mm}p{125mm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Req} & \textbf{Verdict} & \textbf{Evidence} \\
\midrule
\endhead
FR-7 & \MET &
\code{data/glossary.json}: 31 terms, each citing its authority --- the Manual of Office Procedure
or SRS FR-11. \code{make glossary} re-measures corpus evidence only and never overwrites a
curated decision. \\
\addlinespace
FR-8 & \MET &
Non-approved renderings are flagged with line numbers and occurrence counts. \\
\addlinespace
FR-9 & \MET &
The approved term is suggested, \emph{except} where a replacement cannot be justified from one
side of the document: {\dev रद्द} (cancelled), {\dev अधिक्रमित} (superseded) and {\dev व्यपगत}
(lapsed) do different things to an order, so ``cancelled'' in an English GR may be a correct
rendering of {\dev रद्द}. Those report the ambiguity and withhold the suggestion. \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\textbf{The finding this module exists for.} Across 2{,}500 corpus documents, ``Government
Decision'' appears in \textbf{1{,}861} and ``Government Resolution'' in \textbf{1{,}021} --- and
the Manual of Office Procedure uses ``Government Resolution'' six times and ``Government
Decision'' \textbf{not once}. The dominant machine-translated rendering is the non-standard one.
No amount of mining the corpus could reveal this, which is exactly why FR-7 asks for a
\emph{controlled} list rather than a discovered one.

\subsection*{3.4 Template Enforcement Engine}

\begin{longtable}{@{}p{11mm}p{15mm}p{125mm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Req} & \textbf{Verdict} & \textbf{Evidence} \\
\midrule
\endhead
FR-10 & \MET &
Structure declared in \code{data/gr\_template.json} from Manual Appendix 10.6. No model call, and
a test enforces that. \\
\addlinespace
FR-11 & \MET &
All four named items verified: preamble ordering (Background $\rightarrow$ Reference
$\rightarrow$ Resolution) as codes T20--T22; financial sanction block; signature headers; and the
budget head, matched on digits rather than separators --- a hyphen-anchored regex finds
\textbf{zero} budget heads in 1{,}500 real GRs, because the corpus writes them space-separated and
in Devanagari digits. \code{2054-00-101-02} and {\dev २२०३-००-८००-०१} both match. \\
\addlinespace
FR-12 & \MET &
Errors, warnings and advisories, each citing the Manual clause that triggered it. \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\textbf{Two rule sets, one engine.} A document \emph{we generate} is held to the full Appendix
10.6 form. An \emph{already-issued} GR read back through OCR is scored differently, because
sections that are images in the signed PDF cannot be read by any text pipeline --- the signature
block appears in 30.4\% of the corpus and the Governor's order line in 2.2\%. Under the drafting
rules 0.2\% of 400 real GRs score compliant, which measures the scan rather than the drafting;
under the issued rules, 45.0\%. Every section not counted is named in the response, so nothing is
silently forgiven.

\section{Engineering guardrails}

The system is designed so that a wrong answer is structurally hard to produce. Each guardrail
below is enforced in code, and most are pinned by a test.

\subsection*{Facts are computed, never generated}

No model call exists in \code{template.py}, \code{references.py}, \code{conflicts.py} or
\code{glossary.py}, and a test asserts the absence of the imports that would allow one. Whether a
draft carries a signature block, whether a citation resolves, whether two figures differ --- each
is settled by a regex or an integer comparison. A language model may \emph{explain} a finding; it
is never the reason the system believes one.

The alignment verdict is the clearest case. Given the findings and asked to judge, the local
model concluded that a document ``remains valid because the cited references are either
unverified or superseded'' --- about a document whose citations had in fact been superseded. The
verdict is therefore computed from the dependency graph and handed to the model to phrase.

\subsection*{Model output is filtered before it reaches the officer}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=6mm,itemsep=1mm]
\item \textbf{Identifiers are withheld from the prompt.} The narrative reviewer is never shown an
      order number, date or line number; citations are identified to it only as \code{[1]},
      \code{[2]}. It cannot corrupt an identifier it never saw.
\item \textbf{Any sentence containing an unexpected digit is discarded whole}, not repaired. A
      mangled file number reads exactly as authoritative as a correct one, so the only safe
      action is to drop the sentence and report how many were dropped.
\item \textbf{Summaries are gated on figures.} Every monetary amount in the document is extracted
      deterministically and whitelisted, including its crore and lakh readings; a sentence quoting
      any other figure is removed. A summary with a wrong rupee figure is worse than no summary.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{The five ways of not finding something are never merged}

A citation that does not resolve is reported under one of five distinct verdicts:
\code{not\_in\_archive} (the only one that is a defect in the document), \code{outside\_range},
\code{other\_dept}, \code{out\_of\_scope} and \code{unverifiable}. Collapsing them into ``missing''
would roughly quadruple the error count and make it false --- on one sample GR, 5 genuine misses
against 12 citations that simply predate the archive. Court orders and Acts are reported as
outside what a GR archive holds, never as missing references.

\subsection*{Abstention is a first-class outcome}

Ambiguity abstains rather than guessing. If a file number matches a series but no member falls in
the date window, the citation is left unresolved rather than snapped to the nearest sibling. If a
citation gives a date but no file number, no resolution is offered --- a date alone cannot identify
an order, because every GR issued that day would match. If a glossary term's correct replacement
cannot be established from one side of a bilingual document, the suggestion is withheld and the
ambiguity is stated.

\subsection*{Evidence travels with every claim}

Every graph edge carries the verbatim Marathi sentence that produced it, and that sentence is
shown wherever the edge is used to justify a finding. Template findings cite the Manual clause.
Glossary findings cite the Manual or state that the entry is a proposal. Semantic hits show the
source GR, its department, its date and a link to the signed PDF on the Government's own site.
An edge whose justification cannot be displayed does not ship.

\subsection*{Degradation is visible, never silent}

If the local model is unreachable, the affected panel says so and shows the computed result
instead. If the corpus is absent, the pages that need it grey out rather than failing on click.
If OCR produced the text rather than a PDF text layer, the interface says which and shows the
confidence, because those warrant different trust. ``The model was unavailable'' is a legitimate
state to display; a fabricated answer is not.

\section{Architecture (SRS \S5)}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lp{112mm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Layer} & \textbf{As built} \\
\midrule
1. Data & mahGRs corpus, PDF$\rightarrow$text$\rightarrow$embeddings, with an EasyOCR
(Marathi + English) fallback for scans. \\
2. NLP engine & MahaSBERT for Marathi sentence embeddings, IndicTrans2 for translation,
Qwen3.5-2B for generation --- all running on-premise, on CPU. \\
3. Vector search & Exact NumPy inner product over a $23{,}977 \times 768$ matrix, a few
milliseconds per query. Chosen over FAISS (``or similar'' in the SRS) because at this scale the
exact scores are cheap, and an SDC deployment needs one fewer dependency. \\
4. Compliance rules & \code{data/gr\_template.json} (Manual Appendix 10.6) and
\code{data/glossary.json} (controlled terminology), both plain JSON and editable without code
changes. \\
5. Frontend & React + Vite dashboard, five pages, bilingual interface, vendored Devanagari fonts
so it renders correctly with no network. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
